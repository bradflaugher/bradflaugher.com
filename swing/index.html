      Left-handed with a racquet: on the forehand your front foot is your right, on the backhand your left. Right-handed with a club: your lead foot is your left.

      Every swing side by side

      Bars run from 1 (smallest, firmest) to 5. Racquet sports cover forehand and backhand; golf is a full iron swing.

      Swings that rhyme

      • Cut: squash, real tennis, rackets, padel. High to low, open face, short finish. Rackets has real tennis's high-to-low, firm-wristed cut in a shorter, squash-sized swing that stops after contact, with no flick, because the ball is hard and fast.
      • Drive: lawn tennis, table tennis. Out in front, low to high, brushing.
      • Compact and firm: pickleball. Swing from the shoulder, no wrist. Padel shares the compact, firm-wristed feel from the cut side.
      • Golf and your backhand: you golf right-handed, so the club swings the same way as your left-handed backhand, with the same hands in the same places: left hand at the top of the grip, back of the left hand facing the target at impact. Borrow that feel. Don't borrow its low-to-high finish; golf hits down.

      Schedule

      Make the days count

      • Spend 10–15 minutes of each session on a solo practice drill before you play points or a round.
      • Put similar swings on back-to-back days where you can (squash then real tennis, pickleball then padel), and keep tennis and table tennis away from the cut sports.
      • Golf the day after squash, real tennis, rackets or padel is slice weather; after tennis or table tennis, watch for a hook. Ten half swings on the range before you go to the tee sorts out either.
      • Film one rally or swing a week, from behind. Compare it with that sport's cue, not with how it felt.
      • A lesson in your main sport every few weeks will move you faster than another five match days.
